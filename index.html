I

Last season — the team

True level

The table understates you by eight places. The football flatters you by two or three.

Everyone at the club has done the first half of this arithmetic: at 56 points Vitesse finish 7th on goal difference and displace Roda JC from the play-offs. The second half is that the underlying performance sat in the eighth-to-eleventh band, ninth of the sixteen clubs that can be promoted.

Both corrections are real, and you need both. The 56 points were close to deserved: every method puts the over-performance between one and four points, which is one result. The gap between 7th and 10th is mediocre underlying performance rather than luck.

Three methods, one answer

Two xG models and the bookmakers agree on direction; none says luck.
Across the division the two xG models’ season totals agree within about 2%; on Vitesse specifically they disagree by 3.5 on xG difference (6.8 on xG for), because one model credits Vitesse’s direct, crossed shot diet (41% of shots from outside the box) more than the other. Quote the rank, carry the range: the gap to the play-off band is “somewhere between six and ten expected goals”, never a single figure.
Six seasons

A recovery to average, not to strength. The collapse was defensive.

Across two divisions and three points deductions, league position is a corrupted read of this club. On xG difference the story is simpler: a top-seven Eredivisie side through 2022/23, one catastrophic season in which the defence fell apart and the attack stood still, then two years climbing back to the median of a second division.

Use xG difference, not the table. Across 85 consecutive-season pairs in this division, xGD rank predicts next season’s league position (r = +0.475) better than this season’s league position does (+0.395). And 10th is neither a floor nor a fluke: of 24 clubs finishing 8th–12th on xGD, ten improved and thirteen got worse. It is not a platform.

How they play

Slower, longer, sideways less often, and a mid-block that defends by shape rather than pressure.

The club’s own season report and the licensed match data describe the same team the independent model does. The Twelve report ranks Vitesse against the other nineteen on seven phases; the licensed Wyscout export gives the match-level detail behind it. The pattern is consistent: a direct, low-possession side that lets opponents have the ball outside the box and rarely inside it, shoots at the first sight of goal, and got the league’s second-worst shot quality as a result.

The edge

The one fixable edge is corners, and it is worth about +3.6 expected goals.

Set pieces are a fifth of the expected goals in this division. You are sixth of twenty at them and first at indirect free kicks — you can already coach a dead ball. You are twelfth at corners, and outside the top ten at corners in four of the last six seasons. That is the hole, and it is the part of the game a wage bill grips least.

It persists. Set-piece xG difference carries year to year at r = +0.74 across 160 club-seasons (better than open-play xG difference in this division), and a club retains 77% of its set-piece spread into the following season. A finishing residual retains 33%. The mechanism is aerial: 42% of aerial duels won against the opposition’s 46%.

The size, said in bands rather than gaps: halve the distance to Willem II and Vitesse move from +2.8 to about +6.4 on xG difference. Over six seasons, clubs in the 0-to-+5 band reached the play-offs two times in eleven; clubs in the +5-to-+10 band, seven times in nine. In points, about two and a half. Say it before it is asked, then rank it third behind the recruitment decision and the contract decisions.

Four cases

What the numbers settle, and what they don’t.

Zonneveld: expect fewer goals, and do not read it as decline

+3.8goals above expected

Nine non-penalty goals from 5.17 expected, the second-largest residual in the division. A player’s finishing residual persists at r = +0.045 year to year: 96% of it evaporates. Expect materially fewer goals next season even if he plays exactly as well. One caveat: at 20 he is in the population where a residual is most likely to contain real development. His chance quality (0.32 NPxG per 90) is the real number, and it is the third best at the club.

Bannis: the striker, and the contract

0.443NPxG per 90 · 15th of 355

Nine non-penalty goals from 1,536 minutes last season, on the chance quality that predicts next season’s goals rather than on a residual. He has started all four matches this season and scored five, from 3.76 expected: over 1.0 NPxG per 90 on a sample too small to quote as a level and large enough to say the role is his. His contract runs to June 2027 with no club option.

Büttner: the exception nobody would guess

Ball progression per 90: best at the club, ahead of every midfielder, at 37, from left-back. Switches of play first among the division’s full-backs (MyGamePlan). He is a specialist, the club’s best at getting the ball forward, in a role that has already been reduced this season.

Schikora: the constant

36of 38 matches

3,144 minutes, all but two league matches, and the highest floor in the squad: on a third-party per-match rating (MyGamePlan) he did not have a bad game all season. A 31-year-old defensive midfielder is the squad’s most reliable outfield player, and on a contract to 2028 he is one of the two ever-presents, with Achouitar, contracted beyond the June 2027 cliff.

Not on this page by design: per-player defensive ratings from third-party models. An on-ball, goal-probability score rewards carriers and cannot see positioning; on the club’s own data it swings by more than a full point between wins and losses for defenders. The team-level picture (opponents reach the final third but not the box) is the reliable one.

III

This season

Three matches

Seven points from twelve on an xG difference of +2.1. Results and performance have met in the middle.

Four matches is a sample too small to support any performance claim; it is a reliable guide to selection. Read the last two together: the 0–3 home defeat to Almere was lost while winning the expected goals and the box entries, and the 4–1 at Den Bosch was won while losing both (out-shot 25 to 12, a penalty saved in the fifth minute). Seven points from an expected 6.7: over four matches the results match the performance, even though no single match has. Vitesse are 7th. Next: TOP Oss at home.

What has changed, per match

Five of the seven move the way this analysis would recommend. Two do not: more crosses than ever, and marginally less pressing. More crosses into a box they now win 57% of is a different proposition from crosses into one they won 42% of. That is the question, and somebody at the club has already found the same problem.

The settled eleven

Match 4 at Den Bosch · back four for the third match running
Seven have started all four: Achouitar, Zumberi, Olde Keizer, Schikora, Schwarz, Sellouf, Bannis. For a club that rebuilt its squad twice in twelve months, that is a settled team. Match 4 brought the goalkeeper back from suspension, Bonnah for Markelo, and Dahbo’s first start. Formation labels differ between providers; the back four since match 2 is the part they agree on.
The summer

Five arrivals: one credible prior sample, three unproven on paper, and on deadline day a centre-back.

The departures took the creative spine: Tahaui’s nine assists, Hoogewerf’s eight goals, Huth’s aerial presence. What replaced them is thin on prior data: three of the four attackers arrive with under 400 minutes of senior football behind them at their previous club. The third-party ratings that accompany those minutes are indicative only, and are treated that way here. What can be said is what the sample sizes are, what each player was signed to replace, and what four matches show. The fifth arrival is different in kind: a 27-year-old centre-back with a full season behind him, on a free, signed on 31 August.

What left

Output lost from the 2025/26 attack

The recruitment model this analysis supports, in three words: chance quality, 25-to-29, length. Fees in this division average about €15,500 and three-quarters of permanent signings are free, so contract length is the only lever that decides whether a signing becomes an asset or an expense: nearly free at signing, impossible afterwards. Volendam give 2.2-year deals and have banked €7.9m in fees.

The shape of the signing

Three findings on this page describe one profile. On deadline day, the club signed it.

Put the recruitment model next to the aerial record and the contract dates and a shape falls out: a centre-back in the 25-to-29 band, with a full season of second-division minutes behind him, on a contract long enough to be an asset. Before 31 August the three at that position were 21, 23 and 23, with one of the three in the June 2027 cliff. The squad did not contain that profile.

Luuk Wouters is that shape: 27, left-footed, 2,924 minutes for RKC Waalwijk last season (seventh most of any centre-back in the league, at the side that conceded the second-lowest expected goals in it), one of six left-footed centre-backs in the 25-to-29 band among the 67 with 900+ minutes, signed on a free, on a deal to June 2028. The club’s own vendor produced a team-fit read on him the week he arrived; its chart is used below and its machine-written prose is not, because it calls his third-strongest axis a weakness.

What one season of data cannot do is pass a persistence test, and what no page can settle is how he plays in this block. What the data settles is narrower: the profile the analysis points to is the profile the club bought, and it cost nothing but contract length.

Does the context transfer?

Where the two clubs sit on the vendor’s style spectra
The defensive block he played in is near-identical to Vitesse’s; the on-ball style was not, last season. His defending numbers travel; his numbers on the ball were produced in a build-up side, and over the first three matches Vitesse have moved toward one (57% possession, 10% long passes), so that context transfers better now than it would have a year ago. One caveat the local data adds: corners were 17.8% of the xG RKC conceded, the second-highest share in the division, and the vendor’s own zone read puts his weakest aerial zone in his own penalty area, so he does not fix the corner problem by himself. The corner count from here is the test.

Three scouting reports

What the club’s own scouting data says about the three, facet by facet.

The club’s Twelve Earpiece account holds a scouting report on each of Wouters, Dahbo and de Carvalho. Each rates the player on nine facets against a pool of peers in his league, and each facet on three to seven underlying metrics with a value and a rank. What follows is that data, with three things added: the size of the pool and the sample, which decide what a rank can mean; what the numbers support and what they cannot; and where the report’s own machine-written text contradicts its own table. The ranks are quoted; the prose is not.

Each fit is judged against how Vitesse are playing this season, not last: four matches, 7th on seven points, an xG difference of +2.1, a back four since match 2 and the same seven starters throughout. On the licensed data for the first three matches the side keeps more of the ball (57% against 48%), plays fewer long passes (10% of passes against 14%), crosses more (26 a match against 17) and wins more in the air (57% of aerial duels against 42%). Three matches is a description of intent, not a settled style; the fits below are read with that in mind.
