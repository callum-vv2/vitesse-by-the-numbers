I

Last season — the team

True level

The table understates Vitesse by eight places. The football flatters them by two or three.

Everyone at the club has done the first half of this arithmetic: at 56 points Vitesse finish 7th on goal difference and displace Roda JC from the play-offs. The second half is that the underlying performance sat in the eighth-to-eleventh band, ninth of the sixteen clubs that can be promoted.

Both corrections are real, and both are needed. The 56 points were close to deserved: both models and the bookmakers put the over-performance between one and four points, which is one result. The gap between 7th and 10th is mediocre underlying performance rather than luck.

Two xG models, one answer

Vitesse, 2025/26 · both agree on direction, and neither says luck.
Across the division the two xG models’ season totals agree within about 2%; on Vitesse specifically they disagree by 3.5 on xG difference (6.8 on xG for), because one model credits Vitesse’s direct, crossed shot diet (41% of shots from outside the box) more than the other. The bookmakers back it up: on closing odds for the 26 matches they priced, Vitesse earned 1.2 points more than the market expected, 9th in the division. Quote the rank, carry the range: the gap to the play-off band is “somewhere between six and ten expected goals”, never a single figure.
Six seasons

A recovery to average, not to strength. The collapse was defensive.

Across two divisions and three points deductions, league position is a corrupted read of this club. On xG difference the story is simpler: a top-seven Eredivisie side through 2022/23, one catastrophic season in which the defence fell apart and the attack stood still, then two years climbing back to the median of a second division.

Use xG difference, not the table. Across 85 consecutive-season pairs in this division, xGD rank predicts next season’s league position (r = +0.475) better than this season’s league position does (+0.395). And 10th is neither a floor nor a fluke: of 24 clubs finishing 8th–12th on xGD, ten improved and thirteen got worse. It is not a platform.

How they played · 2025/26

Last season: slower, longer, sideways less often, and a mid-block that defended by shape rather than pressure.

Everything in this section describes the completed 2025/26 season, the team Vitesse were. The first four matches of 2026/27 already look different on several of these measures, and that comparison is in Part III. The Twelve Football season report and the licensed Wyscout match data describe the same team the independent model does. The Twelve report ranks Vitesse against the other nineteen on seven phases; the licensed Wyscout export gives the match-level detail behind it. The pattern is consistent: a direct, low-possession side that let opponents have the ball outside the box and rarely inside it, shot at the first sight of goal, and got the league’s second-worst shot quality as a result.

The edge

The one fixable edge is corners, and it is worth about +3.6 expected goals.

Set pieces are a fifth of the expected goals in this division. Vitesse are sixth of twenty at them and first at indirect free kicks, so the club can already coach a dead ball. They are twelfth at corners, and outside the top ten at corners in four of the last six seasons. That is the hole, and it is the part of the game a wage bill grips least.

It persists. Set-piece xG difference carries year to year at r = +0.74 across 160 club-seasons (better than open-play xG difference in this division), and a club retains 77% of its set-piece spread into the following season. A finishing residual retains 33%. The mechanism is aerial: 42% of aerial duels won against the opposition’s 46%.

The size, said in bands rather than gaps: halve the distance to Willem II and Vitesse move from +2.8 to about +6.4 on xG difference. Over six seasons, clubs in the 0-to-+5 band reached the play-offs two times in eleven; clubs in the +5-to-+10 band, seven times in nine. In points, about two and a half. Say it before it is asked, then rank it third behind the recruitment decision and the contract decisions.

Four cases

What the numbers settle, and what they don’t.

Zonneveld: expect fewer goals, and do not read it as decline

+3.8goals above expected

Nine non-penalty goals from 5.17 expected, the second-largest residual in the division. A player’s finishing residual persists at r = +0.045 year to year: 96% of it evaporates. Expect materially fewer goals next season even if he plays exactly as well. One caveat: at 20 he is in the population where a residual is most likely to contain real development. His chance quality (0.32 NPxG per 90) is the real number, and it is the third best at the club.

Bannis: the striker, and the contract

0.443NPxG per 90 · 15th of 355

Nine non-penalty goals from 1,536 minutes last season, on the chance quality that predicts next season’s goals rather than on a residual. He has started all four matches this season and scored five, from 3.76 expected: over 1.0 NPxG per 90 on a sample too small to quote as a level and large enough to say the role is his. His contract runs to June 2027 with no club option; the calendar below is where that sits.

Büttner: the exception nobody would guess

Ball progression per 90: best at the club, ahead of every midfielder, at 37, from left-back. Switches of play first among the division’s full-backs (MyGamePlan). He is a specialist, the club’s best at getting the ball forward, in a role that has already been reduced this season.

Schikora: the constant

36of 38 matches

3,144 minutes, all but two league matches, and the highest floor in the squad: on a third-party per-match rating (MyGamePlan) he did not have a bad game all season. A 31-year-old defensive midfielder is the squad’s most reliable outfield player, and on a contract to 2028 he is one of the two ever-presents, with Achouitar, contracted beyond the June 2027 cliff.

Not on this page by design: per-player defensive ratings from third-party models. An on-ball, goal-probability score rewards carriers and cannot see positioning; on Vitesse’s 2025/26 data it swings by more than a full point between wins and losses for defenders. The team-level picture (opponents reach the final third but not the box) is the reliable one.

This season

2026/27 · four matches

Seven points from twelve on an xG difference of +2.1. Results and performance have met in the middle.

Four matches is a sample too small to support any performance claim; it is a reliable guide to selection. Read the last two together: the 0–3 home defeat to Almere was lost while winning the expected goals and the box entries, and the 4–1 at Den Bosch was won while losing both (out-shot 25 to 12, a penalty saved in the fifth minute). Seven points from an expected 6.7: over four matches the results match the performance, even though no single match has. Vitesse are 7th. Next: TOP Oss at home.

The summer · 2026/27 window

Four attackers, three of them on under 400 prior minutes, then on deadline day the centre-back this analysis points to.

Three findings on this page describe a signing. The one fixable edge is corners, and its mechanism is aerial: Vitesse won 42% of their aerial duels in 2025/26 against their opponents’ 46%, so the fix is a player as much as a training session. The recruitment model that survives testing has three parts: chance quality, the 25-to-29 age band and contract length. Fees in this division average about €15,500 and three-quarters of permanent signings are free, so length is the only lever that decides whether a signing becomes an asset or an expense, nearly free at signing and impossible afterwards. Volendam give 2.2-year deals and have banked €7.9m in fees. And before 31 August the three centre-backs were 21, 23 and 23, with one in the June 2027 cliff. Together that describes a centre-back who wins headers, aged 25 to 29, on a deal of two years or more. The squad did not contain that profile. The same analysis puts the recruitment process and the contract decisions ahead of any single player, and a separate vendor report (MyGamePlan) named a proven centre-forward as the first gap.

The departures set the other need. They took the creative spine: Tahaui’s ten assists (on the independent model), Hoogewerf’s eight goals, Huth’s aerial presence. What replaced them is thin on prior data. Three of the four attackers arrived with under 400 minutes of senior football behind them at their previous club, and the third-party ratings that accompany those minutes are indicative only. The fifth arrival is different in kind: a 27-year-old centre-back with a full season behind him, on a free, signed on 31 August, the profile described above.

Every card separates two records. Before Vitesse is what the player did at his previous club, with the league and season named; it is the only basis on which he could have been scouted. At Vitesse is his minutes, starts and output in the four league matches to 28 August. The two are never added together. Three of the five (Wouters, Dahbo, de Carvalho) have a Twelve Earpiece scouting report, and each is read in full in the next section.

Three scouting reports

Wouters, Dahbo and de Carvalho, facet by facet, and the fit to the side Vitesse are now.

Twelve Earpiece scouting reports exist for each of the three. Each rates the player on nine facets against a pool of peers in his league, and each facet on three to seven underlying metrics with a value and a rank. What follows is that data, with three things added: the size of the pool and the sample, which decide what a rank can mean; what the numbers support and what they cannot; and where the report’s own machine-written text contradicts its own table. The ranks are quoted; the prose is not.

Two of the three reports rate the player before he joined: Wouters on his 2025/26 season at RKC Waalwijk, Dahbo on his 2026 Allsvenskan minutes at Häcken. The third rates de Carvalho on his Vitesse minutes, the first four matches of 2026/27. Each block is labelled with which, carries both records from his card, and the radar caption repeats the period.

Each fit is judged against how Vitesse are playing this season: four matches, 7th on seven points, an xG difference of +2.1, a back four since match 2 and the same seven starters throughout. On the licensed data for the first three matches the side keeps more of the ball (57% against 48% in 2025/26), plays fewer long passes (10% of passes against 14%), crosses more (26 a match against 17) and wins more in the air (57% of aerial duels against 42%). Three matches describe an intent; the fits below are read with that in mind.

The calendar

The contract cliff is the cheapest thing on this list, and leverage runs out on 1 January.

Fourteen of the twenty-seven contracts expire in June 2027, including six of the settled eleven. Leverage over every one of them is gone on 1 January, not in June. Bucket them in September.

The head coach’s contract runs to summer 2027 too, the first continuity decision, and it resolves in the same window. A separate count on the wider registered list gives eighteen of thirty-three; the denominators differ, the argument does not: more than half the squad and most of the first eleven are out of contract in ten months.
